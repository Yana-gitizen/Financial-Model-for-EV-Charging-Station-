1. Is this EV charging station economically viable?

Yes — but it’s a utilization-driven business, not a pure pricing play.

From the model:

Unlevered IRR: ~23.9%

Levered IRR: ~34.6%

Payback period: ~5.7 years (unlevered), 4.4 years (levered)

EBITDA margin stabilizes: ~41–43% mid-life

Exit multiple assumed: 4.5× EV/EBITDA

This is economically viable only if vehicle throughput materializes as projected. Pricing alone cannot save under-utilized chargers.

 
 Strategic takeaway:

This is a traffic monetization asset, not an electricity retail business.

2. What actually drives profitability?
Ranked value drivers (from sensitivity + break-even analysis):

Avg. daily EVs served

Charger utilization / idle time

Charging price per kWh

CAPEX per charger

Operating expenses (secondary impact)

Break-even:

~29 EVs/day → EBITDA break-even

~53 EVs/day → Business plan break-even (comfortable margin)

At target scale, the model assumes ~80 EVs/day, which is why returns look strong.

 Strategic takeaway:

Site selection and traffic capture matter more than charger technology or marginal pricing tweaks.

3. Is the charger mix optimal?
Current mix (simplified):

DC Fast (CHAdeMO + CCS): Revenue and EBITDA backbone

AC Level 1 & 2: Low revenue density, high idle hours

From electricity sales volumes:

DC fast chargers contribute disproportionately more kWh + revenue

AC chargers dilute capital efficiency unless bundled with long-dwell use cases

Strategic recommendation:

Over time, reallocate future CAPEX toward DC fast

Treat AC chargers as:

Loss leaders

Amenities for retail/parking integration

Demand feeders into DC fast upgrades

4. How defensible is this business?

Moderately defensible, but not naturally monopolistic.

Defensibility comes from:

Land ownership (huge advantage vs rented sites)

Grid + solar hybrid power (cost stability)

Ancillary revenues (~20–25% of gross profit)

Weak defensibility areas:

Chargers themselves are commoditized

Switching costs for EV drivers are low

Pricing power capped by nearby stations

Strategic takeaway:

The moat is location + throughput + ecosystem, not hardware.

5. Do ancillary revenues materially matter?

Yes — they improve resilience, not headline IRR.

Lifetime revenue mix:

EV charging: ~75%

Convenience store + ads + services: ~25%

Ancillary gross margins often >50%

They:

Absorb fixed OPEX

Smooth volatility if charging demand lags

Improve valuation optics at exit

 
 Strategic takeaway:

Ancillary revenue won’t make you rich — but it will keep you alive during adoption volatility.

6. What are the biggest risks?
Model-implied risks (not hypothetical):

Overestimated EV traffic growth

Idle time per charger higher than planned

CAPEX overruns on land / solar

Local competition compressing utilization

Regulatory or grid constraints

The sensitivity tornado shows IRR collapses fastest when:

EV/day drops by 10–20%

Utilization assumptions break

Strategic takeaway:

This business fails slowly, then suddenly — when utilization dips below threshold.

7. Is the exit valuation realistic?

Exit assumptions:

EV/EBITDA multiple: 4.5×

Equity value at exit: ~$9.9M

10-year hold

This is reasonable but not generous:

Infrastructure funds may pay 4–6×

Strategic buyers care more about network density than single-site EBITDA

Strategic recommendation:

Build optionality for:

Roll-up into a regional network

Utility or oil-company acquisition

REIT-style infrastructure exit

Bottom line (executive summary)

This model works if and only if:

Location captures sustained EV traffic

DC fast chargers stay busy

CAPEX discipline is enforced

Ancillary revenues are treated as buffers, not miracles
